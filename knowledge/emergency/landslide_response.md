# Landslide Response

SecureMesh Demo Operational Knowledge. Written for demonstration; not official
agency guidance.

## Warning signs

Landslides usually announce themselves. New cracks in ground, walls or roads;
doors and windows that begin to jam; fences, poles and trees leaning downslope;
water emerging from a slope face where it did not before; and a rumbling that
grows louder are all indications that movement is under way.

A slope that has already failed once is more likely to fail again, not less.
Treat the area around a fresh scarp as active.

## Immediate actions

Move laterally out of the path, not downhill ahead of it. Debris travels faster
than a person can run, and the safe direction is across the flow line toward
higher stable ground.

Account for the team, then establish an observation point with a clear view of
the slope and an escape route that does not cross the debris. Assign one person
to watch the slope and nothing else.

## Assessment

Record the extent of the slip, whether material is still moving, what is
buried, and what lies uphill that could release next. Note whether the slide has
dammed a watercourse: a debris dam that later fails produces a flood wave
downstream, and that risk is often missed while attention is on the slide
itself.

## Access and routes

Assume any road crossing the slide is unusable regardless of how it looks.
Undermining is invisible from the surface, and vehicles have been lost on
sections that appeared intact. Establish an alternative route and report it,
because the plan held by the receiving team probably assumes the road that is
now gone.

## Escalate when

Movement is continuing, a watercourse is blocked, a structure is in the path, a
road is severed with people beyond it, or anyone is unaccounted for.
