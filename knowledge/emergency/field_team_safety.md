# Field Team Safety

SecureMesh Demo Operational Knowledge. Written for demonstration; not official
agency guidance.

## Before deploying

Brief the task, the hazards, the route, the communications plan and the
fallback. Confirm each member has heard the same brief rather than assuming
distribution equals receipt.

Record who is deployed, where, with what equipment, and when they are expected
to report. A team whose expected report time is not written down cannot be
noticed as overdue.

## Working

Do not work alone in a hazard area. Keep the team within sight or within
confirmed radio contact, and re-establish contact on a schedule rather than only
when something happens.

Report position changes. A team that moves without reporting is, from the
outside, indistinguishable from a team in difficulty.

Rest and rotate. Fatigue produces the errors that turn a manageable situation
into a casualty, and it accumulates faster in heat, cold and noise than people
estimate.

## Personal protection

Wear what the hazard requires, not what is convenient: head, eye, hand and foot
protection, and high-visibility clothing where vehicles or aircraft operate.
Carry water and use it. Dehydration degrades judgement well before it produces
obvious symptoms.

## Stopping work

Any team member may stop the task if they judge it unsafe, and that decision is
supported without argument at the time. Review afterwards, not during.

Withdraw when conditions exceed the brief, when communications are lost beyond
the agreed fallback, when light or weather closes in, or when anyone is
unaccounted for.

## After

Account for everyone and every item of equipment. Report injuries and near
misses, including those that caused no harm: a near miss is the same event with
a better outcome, and it is the cheapest warning available.
