# Lightning Safety

SecureMesh Demo Operational Knowledge. Written for demonstration; not official
agency guidance.

## The rule

When thunder roars, go indoors. If the gap between the flash and the thunder is
thirty seconds or less, the storm is close enough to strike your position. Wait
thirty minutes after the last thunder before resuming outdoor work: most
casualties occur before the storm arrives or after people assume it has passed.

## Safe and unsafe shelter

A substantial building with wiring and plumbing, or a fully enclosed metal
vehicle with the windows up, is safe. Inside, stay off corded phones, away from
plumbing, and back from windows and exterior doors.

Open shelters, picnic structures, tents, sheds and the lee of a rock face are
not safe. They stop rain and do nothing about current.

## If caught in the open

Move off ridges, summits and open ground and away from isolated tall objects.
Do not shelter under a lone tree, which is among the most dangerous places
available. Get out of and away from water immediately.

Spread the group out so a single strike cannot involve everyone, keeping people
within sight. Crouching offers little protection; keep moving toward real
shelter rather than stopping to assume a position.

## Casualties

A person struck by lightning carries no charge and is safe to touch
immediately. Begin resuscitation without delay: cardiac arrest is the usual
cause of death and the outcome is strongly time-dependent. Check for burns at
entry and exit points, and for hearing and eye injury.

## Equipment

Disconnect and shut down exposed antennas and mast-mounted equipment before the
storm arrives, not during it. Record any equipment that was live during a nearby
strike so it can be checked before it is trusted again.
