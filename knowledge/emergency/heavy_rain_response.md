# Heavy Rain Response

SecureMesh Demo Operational Knowledge. Written for demonstration; not official
agency guidance.

## Immediate actions

Heavy rain response begins with accounting for people. Confirm the location of
every field team member by radio before anyone moves, and record the count. Move
personnel and vehicles off low ground, dry riverbeds and culvert approaches,
because these fill faster than the surrounding terrain and give little warning.

Do not attempt to cross moving water on foot or by vehicle. Six inches of moving
water is enough to take a person off their feet, and two feet will float most
vehicles. Depth is not the deciding factor; the road surface underneath may
already have been scoured away.

## Assessment

Record rainfall duration and intensity, not just the fact that it is raining.
Sustained rain on already saturated ground is the condition that produces
flooding and slope failure, so note how long the ground has been wet. Watch for
water that is rising rather than standing, and for water carrying debris or
changing colour, which indicates it is moving material from upstream.

## Protecting equipment and communications

Keep radios, batteries and node hardware above the highest water mark you can
identify, and assume any mark you can see has been exceeded before. Heavy rain
degrades line-of-sight radio and can bring down mains power, so switch to
battery reserve early rather than after an outage, and record the reserve level.

## Reporting

Report the location, the depth if it can be measured safely, whether the water
is rising or falling, and whether any route is now impassable. A report of
flooding with no direction of travel does not tell the receiving team whether to
move now or wait.

## Escalate when

Water is rising faster than roughly one foot per hour, a route out of the area
is threatened, a structure is taking water, or any person is unaccounted for.
Escalate on the first of these, not the third.
