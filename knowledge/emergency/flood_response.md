# Flood Response

SecureMesh Demo Operational Knowledge. Written for demonstration; not official
agency guidance.

## Priorities in order

Life safety, then containment of further exposure, then property. Flood response
inverts the usual instinct to save equipment first, because floodwater rises
while people are deciding.

Move to high ground before a route is cut, not after. A team that waits for
certainty about the water level is usually deciding between worse options by the
time it has that certainty.

## Water hazards

Treat all floodwater as contaminated. It carries sewage, fuel, agricultural
chemicals and sharp debris, and skin contact with open wounds is a real
infection risk. Wash and cover any break in the skin before re-entering.

Assume every downed line is live and every submerged electrical fitting is
energised. Do not enter standing water in a building until supply has been
isolated at the main, and do not restore supply until a competent person has
inspected it.

## Structures

A building that has taken water is not safe simply because the water has gone.
Foundations undermined by scour, floors softened, and contents shifted against
doors are all common. Enter only when a structural assessment has been made, and
brief anyone entering that the interior layout may have changed.

## Sheltering and evacuation

If evacuation is ordered, move upward and outward: upper floors buy time, but
only leaving the flood area removes the hazard. Never shelter in a basement or a
sealed attic without an exit to the roof.

Record who has been moved, from where, and to where. An evacuation with no roll
call produces search tasking later that consumes more resource than the
evacuation did.

## Recovery

Document conditions before disturbing them, photograph water lines, and keep a
timeline. Do not drink from or cook with any supply that may have been
inundated until it has been declared safe.
