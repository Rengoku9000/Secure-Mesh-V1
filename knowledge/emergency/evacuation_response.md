# Evacuation

SecureMesh Demo Operational Knowledge. Written for demonstration; not official
agency guidance.

## Deciding

Evacuate early. The cost of an evacuation that turns out to have been
unnecessary is far lower than the cost of one begun too late, and late
evacuations put people on the road inside the hazard.

Define the area being cleared, the destination, and the route before the order
goes out. An evacuation without a named destination produces people who leave
and then stop somewhere no safer.

## Order of movement

Move those who need the most assistance first: people with limited mobility,
medical dependencies, or no transport of their own. They take the longest and
they have the least ability to respond to a shortened warning.

Keep households and dependent groups together. Separating them creates search
tasking that consumes more resource than moving them together would have.

## Accounting

Record who has left, from what address or sector, by what route, and to which
destination. This roll is the single most valuable product of an evacuation: it
is what turns an unaccounted-for person into a confirmed movement, and it
prevents a search of an area that is already empty.

Mark cleared structures using an agreed marking scheme and record the time of
clearance.

## Routes

Confirm the route is passable before directing anyone along it, and identify an
alternative. Report route closures immediately: a route that fails during an
evacuation strands people in the hazard area.

Control the flow so the route does not saturate. A blocked evacuation route is
functionally the same as a closed one.

## Returning

Nobody returns until the hazard has passed and the area has been assessed:
structures, utilities, water and access. Announce a return through the same
channel that carried the evacuation order, so the two cannot be confused.
