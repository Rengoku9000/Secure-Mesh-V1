# Communications Failure

SecureMesh Demo Operational Knowledge. Written for demonstration; not official
agency guidance.

## Recognising it

A failure is not always obvious: a link that appears up but delivers nothing,
one direction working while the other does not, or a repeater carrying traffic
from one team and not another. Test rather than assume, and record the time the
last confirmed exchange took place.

## Fallback order

Work down a pre-agreed order rather than improvising: primary radio net, then
alternative net or channel, then direct simplex between teams, then the mesh,
then physical messenger. Everyone must know the order in advance, because the
moment it is needed is the worst moment to agree it.

Agree in advance when teams will attempt contact, for instance on the hour, so
that two teams both listening at different times do not each conclude the other
is lost.

## Operating on the mesh

SecureMesh is designed for exactly this condition. Record incidents locally and
let them replicate when a peer is in range; do not hold a report back waiting
for connectivity. Keep entries short and factual, since they may be read by
someone with no other information.

Nothing is lost by writing a record that cannot be sent yet. It is signed,
stored and queued, and it replicates when a peer appears.

## Power and hardware

Confirm battery state before concluding the network has failed: a flat handset
and a failed repeater look identical from one end. Check antennas and
connectors, which fail far more often than radios do. Switch to battery reserve
early and record the level.

## Reporting

When contact is restored, report the duration of the outage, what was affected,
what fallback was used, and any traffic that was not delivered. An outage with
no record of what went undelivered leaves the receiving team unable to tell what
it is missing.
