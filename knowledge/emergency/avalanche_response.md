# Avalanche Response

SecureMesh Demo Operational Knowledge. Written for demonstration; not official
agency guidance.

## Survival window

Burial survival falls steeply with time. The great majority of survivors are
recovered within the first fifteen minutes, and the odds decline sharply after
about thirty. This is why companion rescue by the team already present, not a
called-in response, saves lives: an external team will not arrive inside the
window.

## Immediate actions at the scene

Appoint one person as leader before anyone starts searching, and make sure
someone is watching the slope above for a second release. A rescue party caught
by a secondary avalanche turns one incident into a much larger one.

Mark the last-seen point of each missing person and the point of entry into the
debris. Search downslope of the last-seen point, concentrating on catchment
areas: benches, terrain traps, the outside of bends, and the toe of the debris,
where burial depth is greatest.

Use transceivers in search mode, confirming every member of the searching party
has switched from transmit. Probe systematically in a grid rather than at
random. Once a probe strike is made, leave the probe in place and dig in from
the downhill side rather than straight down, which is slower and risks striking
the buried person.

## On locating a casualty

Clear the airway first and protect it while excavating the chest. Handle the
casualty gently: a deeply buried, severely cold casualty can arrest on rough
movement. Insulate from the ground immediately, because conduction into snow
continues to remove heat after extraction.

## Reporting

Report the number missing, the number recovered, the burial depth if known, the
time of the release, and the current slope conditions. Time of release is the
figure that lets a receiving team judge the window.

## Do not

Do not send an unequipped party onto the debris. Do not re-enter the runout to
recover equipment while any risk of a second release remains. Do not stand
people in a group at the toe of the slope while searching above them.
