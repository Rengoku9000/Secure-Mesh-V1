# Earthquake Response

SecureMesh Demo Operational Knowledge. Written for demonstration; not official
agency guidance.

## During shaking

Drop, cover and hold on. Get under sturdy furniture if it is within reach, cover
the head and neck, and stay put until the shaking stops. Moving during shaking
is how most injuries happen: people are struck by falling material while trying
to reach an exit.

If outdoors, move clear of buildings, walls, poles and overhead lines, then stay
in the open. Do not shelter in a doorway of a modern building; it offers no more
protection than the rest of the structure and often less.

## Immediately after

Expect aftershocks and expect them to bring down what the main shock weakened.
Account for the team, then check for injuries, fire and gas or fuel leaks in
that order. Shut off supply at the main if a leak is suspected, and do not use
an open flame or an electrical switch until the area has been ventilated.

## Structures

Do not re-enter a damaged building to recover equipment. Damage that matters,
such as sheared columns, failed connections, or a soft storey, is often not
visible from the outside, and the structure has already been loaded once.

## Communications

Cell and mains infrastructure commonly fail in the first hours, and this is the
condition SecureMesh is built for. Move to the mesh, keep messages short, and
record incidents locally on the assumption they will replicate later rather than
now.

## Reporting

Report structural damage by building and by type of damage, the number of
casualties, whether routes are open, and whether utilities are compromised. Note
the time of the main shock. Report aftershocks separately rather than folding
them into the original report.
