# Road Blockage Response

SecureMesh Demo Operational Knowledge. Written for demonstration; not official
agency guidance.

## First actions

Stop short of the blockage and assess before approaching. Whatever closed the
road may still be active: a slope may still be moving, water may still be
rising, and a fallen tree may be resting on a live line.

Protect the approach so following vehicles do not drive into it. Place warning
well back of the obstruction, allowing for stopping distance in the conditions,
and on a bend allow for sight lines rather than distance alone.

## Assessment

Record what is blocking the road, how long a section is affected, whether the
carriageway underneath is intact, and whether the blockage is stable or
worsening. Note whether it is passable on foot, by a light vehicle, or not at
all. Reporting a road as blocked, with nothing further, does not tell a
receiving team whether a walking casualty route exists.

Identify what is now cut off. A blockage matters chiefly because of what is
beyond it: settlements, teams, medical facilities, or the only route to them.

## Alternative routes

Establish and report a diversion, with its constraints: width, weight, gradient
and surface. A diversion that a light vehicle can use and a truck cannot must be
reported that way, or the wrong vehicle will be sent.

Where no alternative exists, say so explicitly and report the walking time
across the obstruction if it can be crossed on foot.

## Utilities

Treat any downed line across the road as live and keep everyone back until the
operator has confirmed isolation. Do not move a tree or pole that is in contact
with a line.

## Reporting

Report location, cause, extent, whether it is worsening, what is isolated beyond
it, the diversion, and the estimated time to clear if that is known. Update when
the situation changes rather than waiting to be asked.
